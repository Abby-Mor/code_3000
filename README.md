Ethics: 

When working with machines in our rapidly evolving, technology-driven world, it is essential to keep ethics and human values at the forefront of AI development. Throughout my university experience, particularly in security and ethics-focused computer science courses, I have developed a strong understanding of the importance of minimizing bias in AI and machine learning models. These systems are not inherently objective; they reflect the data and assumptions we provide, which makes careful design and oversight critical. When explaining AI to a friend studying health sciences, I compared it to an infant. Just as a child learns from its surroundings, AI systems learn from the data they are given. Even widely used tools like ChatGPT evolve based on interactions and inputs over time. This comparison highlights an important idea: AI is not simply a static piece of code, but a dynamic system shaped by human influence. Developers must approach AI with a sense of responsibility, ensuring that what we “teach” these systems promotes fairness, accountability, and trust. Many AI applications, such as those using facial recognition, have shown racial and gender bias—just one example of human stereotypes seeping into code. We have learned from history that health systems were largely designed around white populations, creating systemic disparities. By acknowledging these patterns, we can take proactive steps to prevent similar inequities from occurring in AI systems, ensuring technology benefits all people fairly.
